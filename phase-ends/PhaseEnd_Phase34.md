## What we believed, what failed, and what we would do sooner (for the retrospective; detail in `docs/decision-log.md` P34 S93)

- **Believed:** the decomp.me step was ready — "paste the regenerated `.s` without its first line". **It failed on the first try:** the
  file was the comparison oracle, not an assemblable target, and the replica had proven the compile, never the paste. **Sooner:** prove
  every third-party step through the third party's own toolchain, on the exact file, and let the proving tool write the paste (done now).
- **Believed:** the flip would wait about a week on GitHub Support. **It passed in a day**, during the previous sub-phase. **Sooner:**
  read a calendar gate the morning after the ticket.
- **Believed:** the tree that passed the flip audit was the tree to publish. **Within hours the owner took two folders out** — the sent
  outreach drafts and the reviewed archive — and a trial removal of the tools archive measured a fresh clone going red. **Sooner:** decide
  the public tree's contents at the consolidation sub-phase; the Archive-index rule (paths, never links) made the late removal harmless.
- **Believed:** pruning untracked scratch was housekeeping. **It was an instrument change:** one audit wrote its probe under the pruned
  tree, and its bare `except` turned the refusal into four "parser defects"; two more red chains followed from citations and coverage
  of the day's own edits. **Sooner:** after any prune, re-run every tool that writes under it; and read every check's exit code — one
  commit here claims "green" from a masked pipeline (named in the log, fixed in the next commit).
- **Cost:** no agents, no model tokens beyond the coordinator; the phase's expense was the five health runs (≈40 min wall) and the browser
  round-trips, and it bought a public repository whose every published number, page and card is generated or verified from the tree.
